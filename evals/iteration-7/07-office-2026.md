## 3. Where LP Returns Come From

**This can't be decomposed from the disclosure, and that is the finding.** The framing does show where the return probably comes from:

- **Exit / value recovery (🟡 likely dominant).** The headline selling point is the price discount to a 2018 sale, which is a bet on the price recovering. A 2018 price is a **pre-2020 baseline** (`GEN-19`), not evidence of value today. If most of the 18% depends on selling the building back toward earlier pricing, the deal is exit-dependent (`GEN-08`, >60% of return from terminal value). The 60% threshold can't be tested without a cash-flow vs exit split → `Q-DS-01`.
- **Leverage (🔴 unknown).** Office NPI earned 3.4% unlevered in 2025. An 18% target over that baseline implies heavy reliance on leverage and/or appreciation (`GEN-07`, financing story). The loan amount and LTV aren't disclosed, so this can't be fired; it is routed to `Q-DS-02`.
- **In-place cash flow (🔴 unknown).** There's no NOI, T-12, or rent roll. Leased-but-dark tenants still pay rent today, but they are the tenants least likely to renew, so in-place cash flow can overstate durable income.

If `Q-DS-01` and `Q-DS-02` come back showing mostly exit and leverage, the verdict should name this a financing story explicitly (`GEN-07` + `GEN-08`; check `EQUITY-06` if the exit cap is below the going-in cap).

## 9. Diligence Checklist

- **PPM / operating agreement:** the fee schedule, waterfall, capital-call and dilution provisions, and GP removal rights, confirmed against what the GP says.
- **Loan documents / lender:** maturity date, extension options and their tests (DSCR, debt yield, LTV), cash-sweep or cash-trap triggers, recourse, rate cap terms. A lender estoppel if available.
- **Independent occupancy evidence:** badge-swipe, parking, or utility data supporting physical occupancy; the building's sublease listings (broker databases).
- **Rent roll and lease audit:** tenant credit, lease expirations through 2028 and beyond, termination or contraction options, free rent outstanding, TI/LC owed.
- **Third-party appraisal or broker opinion of value:** current value on today's NOI and an office cap rate, not the 2018 comparison. Plus submarket vacancy, sublease availability, and absorption from an independent research source.
- **Property condition / capex report:** what it costs to keep a Class A building competitive.
- **Sponsor background:** SEC / FINRA / state searches, litigation search, references from LPs in prior *realized* deals.
- **Prior 2018 transaction:** who sold, at what price, and why. Is the current seller a distressed lender or owner, and what does the discount reflect?

## 10. Verdict

**Pass as presented — insufficient disclosure**
*Screening output only, not investment advice.*

**Reasoning.** Office is a *variable* class, so the only valid benchmark is the deal's own underwriting, and none was provided. The materials leave out the disclosures `01` calls essential for office (physical occupancy, sublease overhang, lease rollover, submarket reabsorption, capex). They also don't say whether the 18% is gross or net, the hold, the fees, the full debt terms, or who the sponsor is. That is too little to underwrite the core return story. What *is* disclosed points to a refi-or-sell event in 2 years (`GEN-09`, `EQUITY-07`) on an asset whose real occupancy is unknown (`GEN-19`), marketed against a pre-2020 price.

**Biggest swing factor:** the **2028 debt maturity**, and specifically whether the building's NOI at that point (after leased-but-dark tenants roll) supports a refinance at the proceeds needed. If it doesn't, LP equity is first in line to absorb the gap.

**What would have to be true to re-screen as Pursue with conditions:**
1. Physical occupancy is close to the 85% leased, with modest sublease exposure in the building and a manageable submarket overhang (`Q-MKT-02`).
2. Leases rolling before or around the maturity are small or already renewed (rent roll, `Q-RISK-03`).
3. The debt has extension options with tests the deal can meet, or a refi-independent path to 2028, and any rate cap covers the period (`Q-RISK-01`, `Q-RISK-02`, `Q-EXIT-02`).
4. The 18% is confirmed **net to LP**, with a disclosed fee stack and waterfall (`Q-FEE-01`, `Q-FEE-03`, `Q-FEE-04`), and a breakdown showing it isn't mainly a recovery-to-2018-pricing exit bet (`Q-DS-01`, `Q-DS-02`), plus a real bear case (`Q-DS-03`).
5. A named sponsor with realized, net-to-LP office exits and meaningful pari-passu co-investment (`Q-GP-02`, `Q-GP-01`).

**Who it might suit, if those check out:** an LP who explicitly wants contrarian office exposure, sizes it as a high-risk position (`01` risk tier: High), can absorb a possible capital call or a total loss of equity, and doesn't need liquidity through the maturity event. It is not a substitute for income-oriented real estate: the 18% here is a recovery-and-refinance bet, not cash flow.
