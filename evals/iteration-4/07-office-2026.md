The numbers clear on paper, but the analog comparison is weak grounding. VNQ is a diversified, largely non-office REIT basket, and the 10yr Treasury (5.18%) currently out-yields it (`05`). Clearing the spread is necessary, not sufficient. For a single-asset office deal, what matters is whether the 18% survives the swing assumptions below, not whether it beats REITs. 🟡

**Unlevered overlay (`05`).** NCREIF NPI office returned **3.4%** total in 2025, the weakest property type, and NPI overall is almost all income with near-zero appreciation. An 18% projected IRR on this asset therefore implies roughly **14–15 points from leverage, lease-up, and value recovery** rather than in-place office income. That is a financing-and-recovery bet, not an income bet (see §3 and §5). 🟡 inferred

**Scenarios.** The GP disclosed no sensitivity (`GEN-13`), so these are directional, not modeled:

## 3. Where LP Returns Come From

Nothing is disclosed on the cash-flow vs. exit split, cash-on-cash yield, or distribution schedule. What the pitch does disclose points one way:

- **The pitch is a basis/recovery story.** "Steep discount to its 2018 sale" sells appreciation back toward a pre-2020 price. That return is realized at exit or refi, not from operations. With office NPI income running in low single digits (`05`), an 18% IRR almost certainly draws most of its return from terminal value and leverage. That points to **`GEN-08` (exit-dependent IRR)** and **`GEN-07` (financing story)**. Both are inferred 🟡 and treated as RED until the GP's decomposition (Q-DS-01, Q-DS-02) shows otherwise.
- **The debt forces a capital event in year 2.** Whatever the hold, the loan has to be refinanced or the asset sold by ~2028. If the plan relies on that refi to return capital or keep distributions going, it is refi-dependent (**`EQUITY-07`**).
- **No distribution schedule.** An IRR with no timing hides whether any cash comes back before the 2028 event (**`GEN-11`**, J-curve).

Rules 3–4 of the skepticism contract apply. The deal is presumptively >60% exit/leverage-driven until the GP shows otherwise.

## 9. Diligence Checklist

- **PPM / operating agreement:** full fee schedule, waterfall, pref, clawback, capital-call and dilution provisions.
- **Loan documents:** maturity date, extension options and their tests (DSCR / debt-yield), rate and cap terms, recourse / guarantees, cash-management triggers.
- **Rent roll and lease abstracts:** WALT, 2026–2028 expirations, top-tenant credit, any tenant already marketing its space for sublease.
- **Physical occupancy evidence:** access-control / badge data or a third-party utilization study.
- **Third-party market data:** submarket vacancy, sublease availability, net absorption, and Class A (trophy vs. A) comps.
- **Current appraisal and replacement cost:** to test whether "discount to 2018" is actually a discount to current value.
- **Property condition report and capex budget:** TI / LC reserves needed to renew or re-lease at the rollover.
- **Lender / refi read:** a term sheet or broker opinion on 2028 refinance proceeds for this asset.
- **Sponsor background:** SEC / FINRA / state records, litigation search, prior office deal outcomes (including any give-backs or foreclosures since 2020).

## 10. Verdict

**Pass as presented (insufficient disclosure).**

**Reasoning.** For a *variable*-class asset, `01` makes the deal's own underwriting the baseline, and none of that underwriting is here. The office essentials that decide this deal (physical occupancy, sublease overhang, submarket, lease rollover, full debt terms) are absent. So are the sponsor, hold, fees, and whether 18% is gross or net. This is not a residual-gap case. The core return story is a recovery-toward-2018 thesis with a year-2 debt deadline, and it cannot be underwritten from four sentences. What *is* disclosed leans negative on the merits: a pre-2020 price anchor (`GEN-19`), a debt maturity almost certainly inside the hold (`GEN-09`, `EQUITY-07`), and an IRR that office's ~3.4% unlevered baseline suggests comes mostly from exit and leverage (`GEN-07`, `GEN-08`).

**Biggest swing factor:** the ~2028 debt maturity. It determines whether the LP gets the recovery upside or funds a paydown, a rescue, or a forced sale first.

**Who it could suit (if it re-screens well):** an LP with explicit, deliberate office-distress exposure, a lock-up it can extend past the stated hold, reserves for a possible capital call, and a sponsor with verified realized office exits through the reset. It is not an income investment, and it is not a fit for an LP who needs distributions before 2028.

**What would have to be true to re-screen:**
1. The sponsor is identified, with realized net-to-LP office exits since 2020 and cash co-invest on pari-passu terms.
2. Physical occupancy is close to the 85% leased figure, near-term expirations are manageable, and sublease overhang in the building and submarket is quantified.
3. There is a credible 2028 plan: extension options with achievable tests, or refi proceeds sized on current office lending standards, with a stated cash-in amount and who funds it.
4. The 18% is confirmed as net-to-LP (or restated as net) with the full fee schedule and waterfall, and a bear case (no refi at assumed terms, higher exit cap, rollover loss) that preserves LP capital.
5. A current appraisal or replacement-cost basis shows the purchase price is a discount to **today's** value, not just to 2018's.
