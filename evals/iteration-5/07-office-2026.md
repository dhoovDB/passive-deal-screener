## 2. Return Stress-Test

**No category comparator applies.** Office is a *variable* class in `01` ("post-2020 occupancy reset; class-A trophy ≠ class-B/C"), and `05` gives variable classes no spread-table row. `scripts/benchmark_comparator.py --deal-type office` returned "none - variable class" and says to benchmark against the deal's own underwriting. That underwriting was not provided, so there is nothing to stress-test it against. The missing baseline is itself a finding. 🟢

**Reference points (context, not a "clears" test)** 🟢 from `05`:
- **Risk-free floor.** 2yr Treasury is about 4.87% and 10yr about 5.18%. Any office equity return has to be compared with a bond that pays more than 5% with no occupancy risk.
- **Unlevered in-place baseline.** NCREIF NPI (all property types) returned 5.00% over the trailing four quarters, almost all of it income. **Office NPI returned 3.4% in 2025**, the weakest property type.
- **What that implies for 18%.** An 18% IRR on an asset class that is earning about 3–5% unlevered means roughly **13+ points must come from leverage and price recovery**, not from in-place operations 🟡. That is a financing-and-recovery bet (§3).
- **Gross vs net.** The 18% is not labelled gross or net. Rule 2 applies: do not compare it with anything until the GP confirms it is **net-to-LP**.

**Scenarios.** No numbers are disclosed, so these are qualitative. Each one is driven by three swing assumptions: (a) **exit price relative to the 2018 level** (the exit cap), (b) **re-leasing and renewal of the 85%** plus conversion of leased space into physically used space, and (c) **refinance terms at the year-2 maturity**.

- **Bull.** Leasing moves above 85%, tenants physically use the space, and renewals hold. The lender extends or refinances at comparable proceeds, and a recovering market pays a price that closes part of the gap to 2018. The 18% is plausible only in this case. 🟡
- **Base.** Leased occupancy stays flat, physical occupancy runs below leased (the `01` risk: "leased space can sit dark"), and rollover costs tenant-improvement and leasing capex. At year 2 the refinance comes in at lower proceeds because office lenders size loans on in-place cash flow. The gap has to be filled by a cash-in refinance funded with LP capital calls or preferred/rescue capital that dilutes the common equity. The IRR falls well below 18%. The size of the drop can't be determined from this disclosure. 🟡
- **Bear.** Tenants roll out, sublease space in the submarket competes on rent, and at maturity the lender will not refinance at any proceeds the equity can meet. The result is a forced sale or handing the keys to the lender at a price below today's discounted basis. **LP equity is impaired or wiped out.** "Cheap versus 2018" does not mean cheap versus the post-reset market, and the 2018 price was set on the pre-2020 baseline that `GEN-19` says no longer holds. 🟡

## 3. Where LP Returns Come From

- **Cash flow.** Can't tell from this: there is no NOI, T-12, rent roll, or distribution schedule. The unlevered office baseline in `05` (3.4% in 2025) suggests in-place yield is only a small part of an 18% IRR. 🟡
- **Exit / recovery.** The pitch *is* the exit: "priced at a steep discount to its 2018 sale" is a thesis that the price will move back toward a pre-reset level. The return is most likely **exit-dependent above the 60% threshold**, which fires `GEN-08` 🟡 (inferred from the stated thesis; confirm with Q-DS-01). The exit-cap assumption is not disclosed, so `EQUITY-06` cannot be tested (must-ask Q-EXIT-01).
- **Leverage.** Leverage has to be present, because there is debt maturing in 2 years, but its amount is not stated. The roughly 13-point gap between 18% and the unlevered baseline points to a financing story (`GEN-07`). Leverage terms are 🔴 unknown, so per the anti-pattern rule `GEN-07` is **not fired**. It goes to the must-ask Q-DS-02.

Read: this looks like a **recovery-plus-refinance bet presented as a value buy** 🟡. The GP has to show the split between cash flow and exit before the 18% means anything.

## 9. Diligence Checklist

- **PPM and operating agreement:** full fee schedule, waterfall, capital-call and dilution mechanics, and any authority to bring in rescue or preferred capital ahead of LPs.
- **Loan documents:** maturity, extension conditions (DSCR / debt-yield tests), rate cap, recourse, cash-sweep triggers. Confirm with the lender directly if possible.
- **Rent roll and leases:** tenant credit, the expiration schedule against the year-2 maturity, free rent and TI concessions behind the 85%, and any dark-but-paying tenants.
- **Third-party occupancy data:** building access or badge-swipe data if the sponsor has it, and submarket physical-occupancy and sublease-availability reports from a broker.
- **Comps and appraisal:** recent office trades in the submarket, to test whether the "discount to 2018" is a discount to *current* value or just the current value.
- **Sponsor background:** SEC/FINRA/state searches, litigation search, and references from LPs in the sponsor's prior office deals, including ones that are still unrealized.

## 10. Verdict

**Pass as presented — insufficient disclosure.** Office is a *variable* class, so the only valid benchmark is the deal's own underwriting, and none was provided. The core return story (buy below 2018, reach 18%) depends on physical occupancy, sublease competition, the exit price, and the refinance at year 2. None of these is disclosed, so the return story can't be underwritten, and that absence is the reason for the Pass. The claimed return, framed against a 2018 price and a leased-occupancy figure, uses exactly the pre-reset baseline `GEN-19` warns about.

**Biggest swing factor:** the **year-2 debt maturity**. If the hold runs past 2 years, the LP's outcome depends on an office refinance sized on in-place NOI, at a point where occupancy has had little time to improve. That is the `GEN-09` → `EQUITY-07` path to capital calls or dilution.

**Re-screen list** (answer these, then run the full screen again):
1. Physical occupancy, sublease overhang (building and submarket), and submarket reabsorption.
2. Hold period; debt amount, LTV, fixed vs floating, rate cap, extension options, and the refinance plan with its fallback.
3. Whether the 18% is gross or net-to-LP; a cash-flow vs exit decomposition; exit-cap assumption; a bear case; distribution schedule.
4. Full fee schedule and waterfall.
5. Rent roll, T-12, lease expiration schedule, capex plan.
6. Sponsor identity, co-investment, and a realized net-to-LP office track record.

**What would have to be true to Pursue:** physical occupancy close to leased with little sublease exposure; debt that can be extended at the sponsor's option past the business plan, or a refinance that works on in-place NOI; a *net* IRR that still clears the roughly 5% Treasury floor by a wide margin at a flat or higher exit cap; and a sponsor with realized office exits and meaningful cash co-investment.

**Who it could suit (if the re-screen checks out):** an LP with a specific view on the submarket, capital held back for a possible call at year 2, tolerance for losing some or all of the equity, and no need for liquidity. It is not a fit for an LP who wants income or for whom this would be a core allocation.
